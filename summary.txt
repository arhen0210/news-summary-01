President Trump declared the war with Iran "militarily over", shifting the conflict from bombing to economic pressure and a maritime blockade. Tehran said the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until seven conditions are met. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, at a G7 foreign ministers' meeting in Paris, said sanctions and the naval blockade are working "so well" that there is "no need" for major combat operations against Iran.

Russia, Ukraine and the United States held their first trilateral talks since February, meeting for about four hours in Abu Dhabi and continuing Wednesday. In Washington, House Republicans blocked impeachment charges against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth 211-204 on a party-line vote; Trump called it an "exoneration". Republicans elected Jim Jordan as House Republican leader, replacing Speaker Mike Johnson in January after sweeping midterm losses, and reelected Senator John Thune of South Dakota as Senate Republican leader. Bernie Sanders said he will not back Chuck Schumer for Senate majority leader, as did Senator Elissa Slotkin and several senators-elect.

In the day's other news, Greta Thunberg arrived at COP31 in Turkey, her first climate summit since 2021, warning that without change food, clean water and functioning infrastructure "will become luxury". Two pile-ups on the Trakia motorway near Yambol in Bulgaria killed 11 people and injured 50, 13 of them seriously. Thousands of students and activists rallied across Indonesia over the government's response to forest and land fires, demanding a national disaster declaration and punishment for those responsible. And the US government received more than $12 billion in revenue from Venezuela's oil exports.
